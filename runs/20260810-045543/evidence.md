## Evidence

1. **Binary population synthesis of the Galactic canonical pulsar population using COMPAS provides the state-of-the-art baseline for pulsar formation channels, but treats ECSN as a sub-channel of CCSN with default kick distributions rather than modeling it as an independently constrained channel.**
   - Source: 2406.11428
   - Relevance: Directly addresses Reviewer Point 1 — the work we differentiate from.

2. **Neutron stars formed in electron-capture supernovae from super-AGB stars receive significantly lower natal kicks than typical CCSN neutron stars, producing a detectable population of wide binary pulsars with low eccentricities.**
   - Source: 2205.03989
   - Relevance: Supports the ECSN low-kick prediction (Reviewer Point 2 — companion observables).

3. **Super-AGB stars (≈ 6.5–12 M☉) form O-Ne-Mg cores that can undergo electron-capture supernovae; the boundary between WD and ECSN outcomes is sensitive to mass-loss and binary interaction prescriptions.**
   - Source: 1703.06895
   - Relevance: Provides the theoretical ECSN progenitor framework and rate uncertainty.

4. **The observed neutron star mass distribution in double NS systems can be used as a Bayesian probe of the supernova explosion mechanism; the Pejcha–Thompson framework maps remnant mass to progenitor structure and explosion energy.**
   - Source: 1204.5478
   - Relevance: Foundational reference for the Pejcha & Thompson mass-based landscape (Reviewer Points 1 & 2).

5. **The neutrino-driven explosion mechanism produces a predictable landscape of neutron star and black hole mass functions, explosion energies, and nickel yields as a function of progenitor zero-age main-sequence mass.**
   - Source: 1409.0540
   - Relevance: The "Pejcha & Thompson landscape" explicitly named in Reviewer Point 1; replaces default COMPAS kick prescription.

6. **Electron-capture supernovae can occur in close binary systems; the ECSN channel in binaries occupies a distinct progenitor mass range from single-star ECSN and produces different binary survival fractions.**
   - Source: 1710.11143
   - Relevance: Justifies explicit binary ECSN channel modeling (Reviewer Point 1 novelty).

7. **Accretion-induced collapse and core-merger-induced collapse of O-Ne-Mg white dwarfs in binaries inside planetary nebulae constitute additional neutron star formation channels with distinct observational signatures.**
   - Source: 2407.03985
   - Relevance: Directly addresses Reviewer Point 3 — AIC as a fourth channel.

8. **The neutron star mass distribution shows evidence for bimodality with a gap near ≈ 1.25–1.5 M☉, consistent with multiple formation channels contributing distinct mass populations.**
   - Source: 1309.6635
   - Relevance: Observational basis for multi-channel mixture modeling (Reviewer Point 2).

9. **Bayesian model comparison reveals a sharp maximum mass cut-off in the neutron star mass distribution, demonstrating that hierarchical Bayesian methods can distinguish competing formation scenarios.**
   - Source: 1709.07889
   - Relevance: Methodological precedent for Bayesian model comparison (Reviewer Point 4).

10. **Asymmetric mass ejection in the early phase of supernova explosions imparts a gravitational tug-boat kick to the newborn neutron star; the kick magnitude depends on progenitor structure and explosion asymmetry.**
    - Source: 1611.07562
    - Relevance: Physical basis for channel-dependent kick distributions.

11. **The standard formation model for double neutron stars, millisecond pulsars, and double black holes involves sequential supernova explosions with recycling; each channel leaves distinct orbital signatures.**
    - Source: 1709.07636
    - Relevance: Establishes the orbital-eccentricity–channel connection used as an additional discriminant (Reviewer Point 2).

12. **Core-collapse supernovae and neutron star formation are reviewed with emphasis on progenitor–remnant mapping and the role of fallback in determining compact object masses.**
    - Source: 1806.07267
    - Relevance: CCSN channel context and rate priors.
