# Evidence

## 2107.04251

- Claim: Electron-capture supernovae (ECSNe) produce neutron stars with lower average kick velocities than iron core-collapse supernovae (CCSNe), but the distributions overlap significantly rather than having a rigid cutoff.
- Relevance: Demonstrates that weak natal kicks exist in observed pulsar populations, supporting a probabilistic distribution model rather than a strict velocity threshold.

## 1802.05274

- Claim: The gravitational tug-boat mechanism explains neutron star kicks through asymmetric mass ejection, and ECSNe can produce variable kick magnitudes depending on explosion asymmetry, including cases like the Crab pulsar.
- Relevance: Directly addresses the Crab supernova as an ECSN candidate and shows that ECS kicks are not universally low but follow a distribution with potential outliers.

## 2205.03989

- Claim: Wide binary pulsars provide evidence for low-kick supernovae consistent with electron-capture origins, with survival rates sensitive to kick magnitude distributions.
- Relevance: Links ECSNe to observable binary population statistics, providing independent constraints on kick distributions beyond single pulsar velocities.

## 1611.07562

- Claim: Asymmetric mass ejection during supernova explosions imparts kicks via the gravitational tug-boat mechanism, with kick magnitude dependent on progenitor structure and explosion dynamics.
- Relevance: Provides theoretical foundation for understanding why ECSNe typically produce lower kicks due to reduced ejecta mass and asymmetry, while allowing for variability.

## 1710.04508

- Claim: Low-mass core-collapse supernovae from 8-12 solar mass progenitors exhibit distinct spectroscopic signatures and explosion properties compared to higher-mass CCSNe.
- Relevance: Characterizes the progenitor channel for ECSNe and lowest-mass CCSNe, supporting the distinction between formation channels based on observable properties.
