# Evidence Cards

## Card 1
**Claim:** Core-collapse supernovae (CC-SNe) of massive stars (M_ZAM ≳ 8–10 M☉) are the dominant formation channel for the bulk of the Galactic neutron star / pulsar population, producing the highest birth rate (~1 per century) and the widest range of natal kick velocities.

**arXiv ID:** 1806.07267

**Relevance:** Establishes the baseline CCSN channel whose rate, progenitor mass range, and kick distribution anchor the three- (now four-) channel mixture model.

---

## Card 2
**Claim:** Electron-capture supernovae (EC-SNe) from super-AGB stars with ONe cores constitute a distinct low-kick channel; their neutron stars are observed as the second-born components of wide, low-eccentricity binary pulsars such as PSR J0737−3039B.

**arXiv ID:** 2205.03989

**Relevance:** Directly links EC-SNe to observable orbital signatures (low eccentricity, wide orbits), providing the key eccentricity-based discriminant for the dedicated binary analysis.

---

## Card 3
**Claim:** Six of the eight (now many more) double neutron stars in the Galactic disk have orbital eccentricities e < 0.27, indicating that their second-born neutron stars received only very small velocity kicks at birth — strong evidence for two distinct NS formation mechanisms (iron CCSN vs. EC-SN/AIC).

**arXiv ID:** 0704.1215

**Relevance:** Classic quantitative demonstration that orbital eccentricity in DNS systems discriminates between formation channels; forms the empirical backbone of the eccentricity-based analysis.

---

## Card 4
**Claim:** Accretion-induced collapse (AIC) of a white dwarf to a neutron star has long been suggested as a natural theoretical outcome in stellar evolution, but there has never been a direct detection of such an event; the predicted optical transient is dim (∼10⁻³ M☉ of ⁵⁶Ni), making it a theoretical prediction awaiting observational confirmation.

**arXiv ID:** 1406.4128

**Relevance:** Authoritative statement that AIC is theoretically predicted but observationally unconfirmed — directly addresses the requirement to frame AIC as a hypothesis to be tested rather than an established channel.

---

## Card 5
**Claim:** Long-orbital-period binary millisecond pulsars (P_orb > 2 d) with highly circular orbits (e < 0.1) can be linked to WD binary progenitors through AIC, providing indirect but testable observational signatures of the AIC channel.

**arXiv ID:** 1901.00547

**Relevance:** Supplies the observable predictions (long P_orb, low e, specific companion masses) that the Bayesian model will use to test the AIC hypothesis, converting it from an established channel into a falsifiable one.

---

## Card 6
**Claim:** The observed velocity distribution of young pulsars, analysed via maximum-likelihood methods on the complete PSRπ sample of parallaxes and proper motions, yields a mean birth speed of several hundred km/s with a well-characterised distribution shape.

**arXiv ID:** 2002.01367

**Relevance:** Provides the empirical natal kick velocity distribution (from proper motions) that enters the hierarchical Bayesian model as a primary observable.

---

## Card 7
**Claim:** Pulsar kick velocities — their magnitudes, directions, and proposed physical mechanisms (hydrodynamic instabilities, anisotropic neutrino emission, magnetic effects) — remain a central diagnostic of the supernova explosion mechanism and hence of the formation channel.

**arXiv ID:** 2412.08446

**Relevance:** Recent comprehensive review establishing natal kick velocity as a primary observable and linking kick magnitude to formation channel; supports the inclusion of kicks in the Bayesian mixture model.

---

## Card 8
**Claim:** Globular clusters retain and dynamically process neutron stars through exchange encounters, tidal captures, and binary recompositions, producing a substantial population of millisecond pulsars whose formation history is decoupled from the isolated-binary channel.

**arXiv ID:** 0711.3001

**Relevance:** Establishes the dynamical formation channel in globular clusters as a physically distinct fourth channel.

---

## Card 9
**Claim:** Globular cluster millisecond pulsars, particularly those in cluster cores, offer distinct advantages for pulsar timing arrays and carry population-level signatures (high MSP density, specific spin/orbital distributions) that distinguish them from the field MSP population.

**arXiv ID:** 2111.14084

**Relevance:** Quantifies the observable distinctiveness of the GC dynamical channel and supports its inclusion as a separable component in the hierarchical mixture model.

---

## Card 10
**Claim:** The known Galactic double neutron star (DNS) population has roughly doubled in recent years, enabling statistically meaningful comparisons of orbital period, eccentricity, and systemic velocity distributions against population-synthesis predictions from different formation channels.

**arXiv ID:** 1904.12745

**Relevance:** Provides the expanded DNS sample that makes the dedicated binary-pulsar eccentricity analysis statistically feasible and anchors the Bayesian model's binary subsystem.

---

## Card 11
**Claim:** Different natal kick velocity distributions applied at the second supernova strongly affect DNS merger times, systemic velocities, and travel distances, allowing observed DNS properties to constrain kick prescriptions and hence formation channels.

**arXiv ID:** 1904.06137

**Relevance:** Demonstrates that DNS systemic velocities and orbital parameters are sensitive to natal kicks, validating their joint use as primary observables in the hierarchical Bayesian model.

---

## Card 12
**Claim:** Close double neutron stars are believed to have experienced at least one common-envelope episode; the details of CE ejection shape the final orbital separation and eccentricity, providing a critical theoretical prior for the binary-pulsar analysis.

**arXiv ID:** 2001.09829

**Relevance:** Supplies the binary-evolution physics (common-envelope episodes) that maps progenitor properties onto the observable eccentricity and orbital-period distributions used to discriminate channels.
