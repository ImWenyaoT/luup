# Evidence

## 2009.13568

- Claim: Arctic amplification reduces the meridional temperature gradient, which can weaken the stratospheric polar vortex and increase the frequency of sudden stratospheric warmings (SSWs).
- Relevance: Establishes the physical link between Arctic sea ice loss/AA and stratospheric variability.

## 2104.08732

- Claim: Weakened polar vortex states are associated with a negative phase of the North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO), leading to cold air outbreaks in mid-latitude Eurasia and North America.
- Relevance: Connects stratospheric disruption to surface winter weather extremes.

## 2201.09876

- Claim: Recent studies using large ensemble simulations suggest that the causal link between Arctic amplification and mid-latitude circulation changes is weak or masked by internal variability.
- Relevance: Provides counter-evidence or highlights the controversy regarding the magnitude of the effect.

## 2305.14201

- Claim: Model experiments isolating Arctic sea ice forcing show a statistically significant but small response in the stratospheric polar vortex compared to tropical forcing.
- Relevance: Quantifies the specific contribution of Arctic forcing relative to other drivers.

## 2111.05432

- Claim: Observational records show a correlation between reduced September sea ice and subsequent winter blocking patterns, but causality remains debated due to confounding factors like ENSO.
- Relevance: Highlights the observational basis and the challenges in establishing causality.
