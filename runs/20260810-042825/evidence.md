| 1 | Electron-capture supernovae (EC-SNe) from super-AGB progenitors (≈6.5–12 M☉) impart characteristically low natal kicks (≲50 km/s) because of the low ejecta mass and near-symmetric collapse, producing a distinct low-velocity tail in the pulsar kick distribution. | 2205.03989 | Directly demonstrates that EC-SN produce a measurable sub-population of wide binary pulsars with low systemic velocities, providing the empirical anchor for separating EC-SN from iron-core CC-SN in the kick distribution. |
| 2 | Super-AGB stars in the 6.5–12 M☉ range undergo off-center carbon ignition and form O-Ne-Mg cores that collapse via EC-SN rather than iron-core CC-SN, with distinct nucleosynthetic signatures (e.g., enhanced [Cu,Zn/Fe] and faint transients). | 1703.06895 | Provides the stellar-evolutionary foundation for the EC-SN channel, including the progenitor mass range, core composition, and observable nucleosynthetic signatures that can distinguish EC-SN from iron-core CC-SN remnants. |
| 3 | The formation history of Galactic double neutron stars (DNS) constrains the relative rates of different NS-formation channels; population synthesis shows that both common-envelope and stable mass-transfer pathways contribute, but the observed DNS population cannot be reproduced by a single channel. | 1805.07974 | Establishes that multi-channel modeling is required for DNS populations, and provides the population-synthesis framework that we build upon with explicit EC-SN and AIC sub-channels. |
| 4 | Key parameters governing double compact-object formation — including the common-envelope efficiency α, the NS kick velocity distribution σ, and the mass-transfer stability criterion — have order-unity effects on predicted channel rates; posteriors must be marginalized over these uncertainties. | 2402.04658 | Motivates our hierarchical Bayesian framework that treats channel fractions as free parameters to be measured from data rather than fixed by prior assumptions, and identifies the population-synthesis nuisance parameters that must be marginalized. |
| 5 | DNS systems can form via consecutive type-II supernovae in massive binaries, with the second SN receiving a kick that shapes the observed eccentricity and systemic-velocity distributions. | 2306.07099 | Provides the iron-core CC-SN baseline for the second SN in massive binaries, against which we contrast the low-kick EC-SN and ultra-low-kick AIC signatures in the same observable space (e, v_sys, P_orb). |
| 6 | BNS mergers can produce supramassive or stable magnetars when the total mass is below the threshold for prompt black-hole collapse; these magnetars have B ~ 10^15 G and P ~ 1 ms, but the rate is bounded by the BNS merger rate (~320 Gpc⁻³ yr⁻¹). | 2606.11299 | Used here only as a rare magnetar-origin channel (not a major pulsar formation path). Its contribution is bounded to ≲1% of all pulsars by the observed BNS merger rate and the requirement of near-maximum total mass. |
| 7 | Population synthesis of millisecond X-ray pulsars shows that the AIC channel produces NS with systematically lower B-fields (≲10^9 G) and shorter initial spin periods than the CC-SN channel, but both channels can populate the observed MSP region of the B–P diagram. | 1509.05027 | Demonstrates that the AIC and CC channels leave distinct imprints in the B–P plane for recycled pulsars, providing one of the key observational discriminants we exploit in the mixture-model analysis. |
| 8 | Comparing supernova rates with the formation rates of single radio pulsars, recycled pulsars, and binaries shows that the majority of isolated radio pulsars must originate from single (or wide-binary) massive-star core collapse, not from binary disruption or AIC alone. | astro-ph/9902181 | Provides the classical rate-budget argument that the CC-SN channel dominates the isolated pulsar population, which we adopt as the physically motivated prior for the dominant channel in our three-channel mixture model. |
| 9 | Magnetar formation channels include (i) core collapse of rapidly rotating, highly magnetized single massive stars, (ii) binary mergers (stellar or WD-WD), (iii) accretion-induced collapse of magnetized WDs, and (iv) fallback-enhanced dynamos; the relative rates remain uncertain. | 2511.06554 | Frames the magnetar sub-population as a multi-channel mixture in its own right, motivating our decision to treat BNS-merger magnetars as a bounded rare sub-channel rather than a major pulsar formation path. |
| 10 | Six of eight known Galactic DNS have low eccentricities (e < 0.27), indicating that the second-born NS received very small natal kicks — consistent with EC-SN or ultra-stripped SN rather than standard iron-core CC-SN. | 0704.1215 | Classic observational evidence for at least two distinct NS-formation mechanisms (high-kick CC-SN vs low-kick EC/US-SN), which we extend here to a three-channel model including AIC. |
