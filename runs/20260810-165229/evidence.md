# Evidence

## 2011.06287

- Claim: Urban socioeconomic outputs exhibit robust superlinear scaling with population size across diverse cities and metrics.
- Relevance: Empirically confirms superlinear scaling (β > 1) in venture capital attraction, establishing the core phenomenon that requires mechanistic explanation.

## 1210.5215

- Claim: Human interaction networks and communication activity scale with city size, suggesting social dynamics as a primary driver of urban scaling independent of physical infrastructure geometry.
- Relevance: Provides the key alternative mechanism (social interaction density) that must be controlled for when testing whether transport network fractal dimension determines scaling exponents.

## 1503.04795

- Claim: Superlinear scaling of gross urban product with population is observed consistently across cities in developed economies, confirming the universality of the scaling phenomenon.
- Relevance: Validates the empirical foundation of urban scaling laws in a well-documented national context, supporting cross-city comparative analysis.

## 2603.30021

- Claim: The interpretation of urban scaling exponents as describing growth processes versus cross-sectional comparisons remains contested, affecting causal inference about scaling mechanisms.
- Relevance: Highlights methodological considerations for distinguishing whether fractal dimension causally influences scaling or merely correlates with it through confounding factors.

## 2001.00311

- Claim: Mesoscopic scaling patterns within cities reveal heterogeneous relationships between urban elements that differ from macroscopic cross-city scaling laws.
- Relevance: Demonstrates why intra-city subdivision analysis cannot substitute for cross-city scaling estimation, as internal heterogeneity reflects different processes than inter-city scaling.
